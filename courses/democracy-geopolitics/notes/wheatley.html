Country cases · Georgia

The Politics of Culture and Identity in Postcommunist States

Jonathan Wheatley (2021) · “A New Political Cleavage in Georgia?”

Wheatley challenges the assumption that postcommunist political competition is primarily shaped by charismatic leadership and clientelistic networks. His argument is that Georgia has developed a distinct cultural cleavage associated with globalisation, national identity and the country's relationship with the West. This division is embedded in social structure and, to a more limited extent, reflected in voting behaviour.

Core concepts

Political structure

Political cleavage

A combination of social divisions, ideological orientations and patterns of political behaviour that becomes reflected in political organisation and party affiliation.

Ideological dimension

GAL-TAN

Green-Alternative-Libertarian versus Traditional-Authoritarian-Nationalist. A cultural dimension contrasting socially progressive and cosmopolitan values with traditionalist and nationalist positions.

Openness

Cultural integration

An orientation favouring openness towards external cultural influences, Western integration and socially liberal values.

Protection

Cultural demarcation

An orientation favouring national and cultural protectionism, traditional authority and resistance to external cultural influences.

Globalisation

Winners and losers of globalisation

Social groups that benefit from global economic and cultural integration versus those that experience dislocation and perceive these transformations as threatening.

Are postcommunist societies ideologically divided?

Western European party systems were historically structured around economic divisions between labour and capital. More recently, cultural issues have generated another dimension of political competition, particularly between cosmopolitan liberalism and social conservatism.

Western European pattern

Two dimensions of competition

Economic divisions between labour and capital historically structured party competition. Cultural issues increasingly introduced a second dimension contrasting cosmopolitan liberalism with social conservatism.

Postcommunist debate

Weaker social foundations?

Communist rule weakened traditional social groups and party-constituency relationships. Political competition was therefore often understood through charismatic leaders and clientelistic networks rather than stable ideological programmes.

Kitschelt and colleagues nevertheless identify overlapping divisions in postcommunist societies: supporters of the former regime versus reformers, economic winners versus losers, and cultural libertarians versus authoritarians.

Wheatley's intervention

The winners-versus-losers cleavage of globalisation deserves greater attention in the post-Soviet context. Globalisation does not affect all social groups equally: different experiences can produce competing political orientations and understandings of national identity.

Georgia's emerging culture wars

Georgian political competition increasingly exhibits a confrontation between religious traditionalism and socially progressive movements. Cultural identity is becoming a more salient dimension of political conflict.

Traditionalist mobilisation

Georgian Orthodox Church

The Georgian Orthodox Church became an influential political actor, opposing certain Western cultural influences and defending its privileged position in Georgian society.

Conservative movements

Cultural protection

Conservative movements mobilised against LGBT rights, secularisation, immigration and what they perceived as Western cultural decadence.

Progressive mobilisation

Civil society and activists

Progressive activists and civil society organisations challenged traditionalist positions, particularly concerning minority rights and secular governance.

Political conflict

Competing visions of society

These tensions indicate that political disagreement is not simply about economic policy or leadership, but also about the cultural direction of the Georgian state and society.

Illustrative event · 2013

Anti-homophobic rally in Tbilisi

The 2013 anti-homophobic rally in Tbilisi resulted in violence against activists. It illustrates the intensity of the confrontation between progressive movements and conservative forces.

Methodology: measuring ideological divisions

Wheatley examines public opinion data collected through the xmamkvlevi Voting Advice Application ahead of Georgia's 2016 parliamentary elections. Rather than assuming that ideological divisions exist, the study tests how policy attitudes cluster into dimensions.

9,245 Cleaned responses in the original dataset
30 Policy statements included
2 Ideological dimensions identified
Analytical method

Mokken Scale Analysis

The analysis identified two ideological dimensions: a dominant cultural dimension incorporating 11 issues, and a weaker economic dimension incorporating three issues.

What makes up the cultural dimension?

Attitudes towards Russia and the West, the Orthodox Church, LGBT rights, national identity and foreign ownership. These issues cluster into a broader cultural orientation rather than functioning as isolated policy preferences.

The ideological configuration

The cultural and economic dimensions are correlated, but they do not follow the conventional Western European alignment. Wheatley identifies a distinctive relationship between economic and cultural attitudes.

Economically left-wing Tends to correlate with social conservatism

Economically right-wing Tends to correlate with socially liberal and cosmopolitan attitudes

The correlation indicates that economic and cultural attitudes are related, but the cultural dimension is substantially more prominent in the ideological structure identified by the study.

From cultural integration to cultural demarcation

The emerging cleavage reflects competing responses to globalisation and different understandings of Georgia's national identity and international orientation.

Cultural integration

Openness and cosmopolitanism

Greater openness towards external cultural influences, Western integration, socially liberal values and international engagement.

Cultural demarcation

Tradition and protection

Emphasis on national identity, traditional authority, cultural protection and resistance to perceived external pressures.

Conceptual representation of the cultural cleavage identified by Wheatley.

Globalisation creates different opportunities and experiences across social groups. These differences can generate competing visions of national identity, cultural belonging and the country's relationship with the West.

Social structure and political preferences

The cultural cleavage is not simply an abstract ideological division. Wheatley finds that cultural orientations are associated with demographic characteristics.

Nationalist-conservative tendency

Social characteristics

Older individuals, women and people with technical education tend towards nationalist-conservative positions.

Cosmopolitan-liberal tendency

Social characteristics

Younger individuals, men and university graduates tend towards cosmopolitan-liberal positions.

These patterns suggest that ideological orientations are socially embedded. Different demographic groups tend to experience and interpret cultural and global changes differently.

Important qualification

Party electorates remain relatively ideologically heterogeneous. The existence of a cultural cleavage does not mean that Georgian parties have become fully programmatic or that voting is determined exclusively by ideological alignment. Charismatic leadership continues to matter.

Core takeaway

Postcommunist political competition cannot be reduced to clientelism and charismatic leadership. Georgia is developing a politically significant cleavage between cultural integration and cultural demarcation. Globalisation produces different experiences across social groups, generating competing visions of national identity and international orientation. These divisions increasingly influence political preferences and party support, even though fully programmatic political parties remain underdeveloped.
