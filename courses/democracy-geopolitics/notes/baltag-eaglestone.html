Democracy & Geopolitics / Moldova / Geopolitical inbetweenness

Furthest away from Russia and ever closer to the EU?

Moldova hedging its bets on alternating alignments and (differentiated) dis/integration

Dorina Baltag & Amy Eaglestone 2025 European Security, 34(4), 624–645 Read the article
Geostrategic in-between Hedging Local agency EU–Russia rivalry Differentiated integration
The argument in one minute

Moldova is not simply trapped between Russia and the EU. It actively uses its in-between position to manoeuvre within their rivalry.

Baltag and Eaglestone argue that Moldova's foreign policy is best understood through the combination of geostrategic in-betweenness and hedging. Its position between competing power centres creates vulnerabilities, but also opportunities for local agency. Across three decades, Moldovan elites have adopted different strategies as EU–Russia relations shifted from cooperation to competition and then conflict. Since 2021, Moldova has moved decisively towards Europe, but domestic polarisation, Russian interference, economic vulnerabilities and unresolved territorial questions continue to complicate this trajectory.

01

The research problem: agency in an in-between position

Eastern European states situated between the EU and Russia are frequently portrayed as peripheral actors caught between competing geopolitical powers. This structural perspective emphasizes their vulnerability and limited autonomy, often treating them as objects of external influence rather than independent political actors.

The authors challenge this tendency. Moldova is certainly constrained by its location, historical dependencies, limited resources and exposure to external pressure. Yet its political elites are not passive. They have adapted their foreign and security policies, selectively engaged with both power centres and used geopolitical competition to pursue national interests.

The central research question is therefore: How, and how effectively, has Moldova deployed hedging geostrategies in response to changing EU–Russia rivalry?

The article uses a qualitative case-study design, drawing substantially on 40 semi-structured elite and expert interviews conducted in three waves: 2013, 2020 and 2024. These are supplemented by documentary evidence, reports, opinion polls and policy sources.

02

The analytical framework

The authors combine two complementary concepts to explain how Moldova navigates its geopolitical position. The first captures the structural condition of being situated between competing powers. The second captures the strategies used by domestic actors to manage that condition.

STRUCTURAL CONDITION

Geopolitical in-between

The externally shaped position of a state caught between competing geopolitical powers. It produces vulnerabilities through geography, historical dependencies, limited resources and external strategic pressure.

AGENCY AND STRATEGY

Geostrategic in-between

The ability of an in-between state to leverage its position to create room for manoeuvre through selective engagement, institutional adaptation and domestic coalition-building.

STRATEGIC REPERTOIRE

Hedging

A deliberate middle-ground strategy that avoids firm alignment with a single power bloc, combining cooperation and competition to manage uncertainty and preserve flexibility.

INTEGRATION TRAJECTORY

Differentiated (dis)integration

Uneven and politically contingent processes of integration with the EU and disintegration from Russia. Economic, political, technological and security relations need not move at the same pace.

The key analytical move is to connect structural constraint with local agency. In-betweenness is not simply a condition imposed on Moldova; it is also a position that domestic actors can strategically navigate.

Hedging beyond balancing and bandwagoning

Conventional accounts often frame small-state foreign policy as a choice between balancing against a threatening power or bandwagoning with a stronger one. Hedging moves beyond this binary. It allows a state to combine cooperative and competitive behaviour towards multiple powers, preserving flexibility when the future distribution of power is uncertain.

Hedging is therefore not necessarily a sign of indecision. It can be a deliberate strategy for managing risk, securing economic benefits and avoiding excessive dependence on either side.

03

Three phases of EU–Russia relations

The article organises Moldova's foreign-policy trajectory around three changing dynamics in EU–Russia relations. Each phase generates a different strategic environment and a different repertoire of hedging tactics.

MOLDOVA'S GEOSTRATEGIC TRAJECTORY, 1990s–2024
1990s–EARLY 2000s EU–RUSSIA COOPERATION

Managing in-betweenness

Moldova accepted external power projections from both Russia and the EU. Its foreign policy sought to preserve sovereignty and access to both relationships without provoking direct confrontation. The Communist Party's alignment with Russia under Vladimir Voronin also reflected this environment.

Strategic silence Manifest emulation Bandwagoning
2003–2021 EU–RUSSIA COMPETITION

Leveraging geopolitical rivalry

As the EU and Russia increasingly sought exclusive spheres of influence, Moldova used its position to extract concessions and maintain relationships with both. Its elites pursued multivectorism and wedging, sometimes weakening Russian influence while retaining economic and political links with Moscow.

Wedging Multivectorism
POST-2021 EU–RUSSIA CONFLICT

Strategic pivot towards Europe

The PAS government's European orientation, reinforced by Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, marks a more assertive rejection of Russian influence. Moldova has pursued EU alignment, energy diversification and stronger security cooperation while undertaking domestic reforms connected to accession.

04

The hedging tactics in practice

The authors identify a range of tactics through which Moldova has navigated its in-between position. These are not interchangeable labels: each describes a particular way of managing external pressure and maintaining strategic flexibility.

Strategic silence

Avoiding direct confrontation with a powerful external actor on a sensitive issue. Silence can preserve room for manoeuvre and prevent escalation, even when the underlying disagreement remains unresolved.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

Moldova's muted demands for the withdrawal of Russian troops from Transnistria during the earlier period helped avoid direct confrontation with Moscow.

Manifest emulation

Symbolically adopting the language, institutions or practices associated with a power centre without necessarily undertaking the deeper reforms or commitments that would constitute full alignment.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

The 1998 Partnership and Cooperation Agreement with the EU and participation in European programmes signalled European orientation while limiting the immediate costs of deeper reform.

Bandwagoning

Aligning with a stronger power to secure short-term benefits or protection. In the article, this is understood as one element of Moldova's broader historical repertoire rather than a permanent strategic commitment.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

The Communist Party's alignment with Russia after coming to power in 2001, including consideration of the Belarus–Russia Union.

Wedging

Exploiting divisions or disagreements between competing powers to prevent one side from establishing exclusive influence. It can create opportunities for concessions and expand a state's strategic room for manoeuvre.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

Moldova's rejection of the Kozak Memorandum in 2003, amid domestic and Western pressure, and the extension of DCFTA arrangements to Transnistria.

Multivectorism

Maintaining relationships with multiple geopolitical centres rather than committing exclusively to one. It allows a state to maximise benefits from competing relationships, although it can also generate contradictory commitments and dependencies.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

The Alliance for European Integration governments combined public pro-European rhetoric with continued reliance on Russian financial networks. Igor Dodon's cooperation with Russia alongside the continued implementation of the DCFTA also illustrates this approach.

Contestation

Actively challenging external power projection and rejecting forms of influence considered incompatible with national interests. This represents a more assertive response than simply managing or avoiding pressure.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

The post-2021 government's rejection of Russian influence, alignment with EU sanctions regimes and stronger cooperation with Ukraine and Romania.

Hard balancing

Strengthening capabilities and external partnerships to reduce dependence on, and vulnerability to, a threatening power.

MOLDOVAN EXAMPLE

Energy diversification away from Russian supplies, including imports from Romania, and increased European support for defence and security capabilities.

05

The strategic pivot after 2021

The post-2021 period represents a significant change in Moldova's foreign and security policy. The PAS government under Maia Sandu has pursued a clearer European orientation, while Russia's invasion of Ukraine has fundamentally altered the regional security environment.

Energy and security diversification

Moldova has worked to reduce its dependence on Russian energy supplies, strengthened energy resilience and secured continued European macro-financial assistance and investment. The country has also developed its defence capabilities with European support, including through the EU's Common Security and Defence Policy engagement.

Closer European and regional cooperation

Moldova's cooperation with Ukraine and Romania has intensified. Hosting the second European Political Community summit in June 2023 also reinforced its diplomatic visibility and geopolitical significance.

The country is therefore no longer merely a territory over which external powers compete. The authors emphasise its growing actorness: its capacity to shape regional developments and pursue strategic objectives of its own.

EUROPEAN ALIGNMENT

Integration with the EU

Accession-oriented reforms, economic and financial assistance, energy cooperation, sanctions alignment and stronger security partnerships.

RUSSIAN DIS-ALIGNMENT

Disintegration from Russia

Withdrawal from Russian-led integration projects, energy diversification, reduced political alignment and resistance to Russian interference.

These developments are connected but not identical. Integration with the EU and disintegration from Russia proceed across different policy areas and at different speeds. The authors therefore resist treating geopolitical realignment as a single, uniform process.

06

Differentiated (dis)integration

A central contribution of the article is its treatment of integration and disintegration as uneven processes. Moldova's European trajectory does not require every policy domain to advance at the same pace. Economic, technological, political and security integration can develop differently according to domestic capacity and geopolitical circumstances.

Initially, Moldova prioritised economic and technological cooperation with the EU through the Association Agreement and Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area (DCFTA), while delaying stronger political alignment. This reflected a pragmatic hedging strategy: gaining economic benefits while limiting the political and reform costs of deeper integration.

The post-2021 trajectory is more politically assertive. The government has moved towards deeper European integration while distancing the country from Russia-led projects such as the Commonwealth of Independent States and the Eurasian integration framework.

Differentiated integration

Uneven participation in the EU's economic, technological, political and security structures. Integration can deepen in certain domains before others.

Differentiated disintegration

Uneven withdrawal or distancing from Russian-led structures and dependencies. Disengagement can progress at different rates across policy areas.

The risk of differentiated disintegration from Europe

The authors warn that Moldova's European trajectory is not irreversible. A future pro-Russian government could prioritise the economic benefits of EU market access while rejecting deeper political integration and the standards associated with EU membership.

This would amount to a form of selective engagement: retaining certain economic benefits while distancing the country from the political and normative commitments of European integration.

07

Domestic constraints and the limits of the European pivot

A strategic reorientation towards Europe does not automatically resolve Moldova's domestic vulnerabilities. The authors identify several conditions that complicate the consolidation of the country's European trajectory.

Political and societal polarisation

Moldova remains divided across political, linguistic, regional and geopolitical lines. These divisions provide opportunities for actors opposed to European integration and make the construction of durable public support for reform more difficult.

Russian interference and hybrid pressure

Russian influence operates through more than conventional diplomatic or military pressure. Disinformation, political networks and interference in electoral processes contribute to domestic instability and challenge the legitimacy of Moldova's European orientation.

Economic vulnerabilities and weak governance

Inflation, unemployment, corruption and limited institutional capacity remain important concerns. These vulnerabilities can undermine public confidence in the government's strategic direction and complicate the reforms required for EU accession.

Transnistria and Gagauzia

The unresolved status of Transnistria and the political dynamics in Gagauzia continue to complicate Moldova's geopolitical positioning. These territories and their relationships with external actors create additional constraints on national integration and security policy.

The diaspora as a source of agency

The Moldovan diaspora is an important counterweight to domestic vulnerabilities. Through remittances, political participation and transnational networks, diaspora communities contribute to the country's European aspirations.

The authors highlight the diaspora's influence on electoral outcomes and the 2024 EU referendum. More than 82% of diaspora voters supported Maia Sandu and the European referendum, helping counterbalance opposition among domestic voters.

Strategic realignment is not the same as consolidated alignment

Moldova's pro-European turn is significant, but its durability depends on domestic reforms, economic resilience and the ability to maintain public support. External alignment alone cannot guarantee successful integration.

08

Theoretical and policy implications

The Moldovan case offers broader lessons for small states navigating great-power competition. The authors identify three particularly important implications.

01

Hedging requires a long-term strategic vision

Hedging can be pragmatic and effective in the short term, but without a coherent long-term direction it risks becoming reactive policymaking. Flexibility needs to serve identifiable national objectives.

02

Domestic reforms are essential

External alignment must translate into tangible benefits for citizens. Governance reforms, institutional capacity and economic improvements are necessary to consolidate public support for strategic choices.

03

Transnational actors reinforce local agency

Diaspora networks contribute financial resources, political support and social connections. Their role can strengthen resilience against external pressure and reinforce European integration.

More broadly, the article challenges deterministic understandings of small-state behaviour. Structural constraints matter, but they do not fully determine foreign-policy outcomes. Domestic actors can exploit geopolitical competition, adapt their strategies and create opportunities for greater autonomy.

The central takeaway

Moldova's experience demonstrates that geopolitical in-betweenness is simultaneously a constraint and a potential strategic resource. Through hedging, domestic actors have navigated changing EU–Russia relations, moving from cautious accommodation and multivectorism towards a more assertive European alignment. Yet the durability of this pivot depends on more than external geopolitical circumstances. Domestic reform, societal support, economic resilience and the management of unresolved territorial questions will determine whether Moldova can translate its strategic reorientation into lasting autonomy and European integration.
