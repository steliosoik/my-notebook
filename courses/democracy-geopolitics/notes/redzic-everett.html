DEMOCRACY & GEOPOLITICS / SESSION 4

Cleavages in the Post-Communist Countries of Europe: A Review

Historical persistence, transitional instability and the limits of frozen cleavage theory

Ena Redžić and Judas Everett (2020)

CENTRAL ARGUMENT

Frozen cleavage theory remains useful for understanding post-Communist party competition, but it cannot fully explain its volatility. Historically rooted divisions, particularly religious, ethnic and geographical ones, tend to persist more strongly than cleavages emerging from economic and political transition. Yet political parties can reorganise existing divisions, incorporate new issues and challenge established alignments.

Core concepts

SOCIAL STRUCTURE

Social cleavage

A division involving social differences, collective identity and political or organisational expression.

THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

Frozen cleavage theory

The proposition that historically established social divisions become institutionalised in party systems and produce relatively stable patterns of political competition.

HISTORICAL LEGACIES

Historical cleavages

Divisions rooted in earlier historical experiences, such as religion, ethnicity or regional differences.

POST-COMMUNIST TRANSITION

Transitional cleavages

Divisions emerging from the transition away from Communism, particularly over economic reform and democratisation.

POLITICAL COMPETITION

Political salience

The extent to which a social division becomes relevant to political competition and voting behaviour.

1. The central distinction: historical vs transitional cleavages

The authors revisit whether Lipset and Rokkan's frozen cleavage theory can explain party competition in post-Communist societies, where democratisation and economic transformation created new political conflicts.

HISTORICAL CLEAVAGES

More persistent

Divisions rooted in earlier historical experiences have generally proved more durable.

  • Religious differences
  • Ethnic divisions
  • Geographical and regional divisions

TRANSITIONAL CLEAVAGES

Less stable

Divisions emerging from the transition to democracy and market economies have often been less durable.

  • Economic reform and privatisation
  • Democratisation
  • Legacy of Communism

Why historical divisions endure

They are rooted in longer-standing social identities and experiences, which can continue to structure political competition.

Why transitional divisions weaken

Issues initially structuring competition can be absorbed into mainstream party agendas rather than producing permanently distinct political camps.

2. Why transitional cleavages are less stable

Economic reform, privatisation and the legacy of Communism initially structured political competition across post-Communist societies. However, these issues did not always translate into durable political camps.

CZECH REPUBLIC · PARTY SYSTEM DISRUPTION

ANO 2011

A relatively established socio-economic left-right dimension was disrupted by the emergence of ANO 2011, which mobilised dissatisfaction with established political parties and challenged existing patterns of competition.

HUNGARY · PARTY AGENCY

Fidesz

Fidesz did not simply inherit an established political division. It actively reorganised political competition around nationalism, conservatism and anti-liberalism.

3. National differences and the limits of freezing

The country cases demonstrate that the persistence of a social division does not necessarily guarantee a stable party system or an unchanged pattern of political representation.

HISTORICAL CLEAVAGES

Croatia and Poland

Historical religious and geographical divisions remain important features of political competition.

HISTORICAL CLEAVAGES

Bulgaria, Romania and Slovakia

Ethnic divisions continue to shape political representation, although their electoral salience and organisational expression vary.

PARTY SYSTEM CHANGE

Czech Republic

Challenger parties have disrupted previously established socio-economic competition.

POLITICAL REORGANISATION

Hungary

Party agency has helped reorganise political identities and establish new ideological divisions.

4. The distinction between social persistence and political stability

The Romanian and Slovak cases are particularly useful for distinguishing the existence of an ethnic division from its actual political influence.

01 · SOCIAL LEVEL

Identity persists

Ethnic identities may remain socially significant within a society.

02 · POLITICAL LEVEL

Salience varies

The electoral relevance and organisational expression of that identity can change.

03 · PARTY SYSTEM

Representation shifts

Mainstream parties may absorb relevant political demands, while electoral alignments become less stable.

Key distinction: Social cleavages may endure even when the party systems representing them change. The persistence of a division and the stability of its political expression are not the same thing.

Theoretical contribution

The review identifies both the continuing relevance and the limitations of frozen cleavage theory in post-Communist Europe.

What frozen cleavage theory explains

Historically rooted social divisions can remain important foundations of political competition across major political transformations.

What it cannot fully explain

Political competition remains susceptible to change as parties reorganise existing divisions, incorporate new issues and challenge established patterns.

Overall takeaway

Frozen cleavage theory remains useful, but it cannot fully explain the volatility of post-Communist party systems. Historically rooted divisions are generally more persistent than those created during the transition from Communism. Yet political parties can reorganise existing divisions, incorporate new issues and challenge established alignments. Social cleavages may endure even when the party systems representing them change.
