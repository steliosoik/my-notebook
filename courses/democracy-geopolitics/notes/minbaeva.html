DEMOCRACY & GEOPOLITICS / SESSION 3

Explaining the Persistence of Informal Institutions

The role of informal networks

Dana Minbaeva, Alena Ledeneva, Maral Muratbekova-Touron & Sven Horak (2022)

CENTRAL ARGUMENT

Informal institutions persist because informal networks simultaneously reproduce established practices and adapt them to changing formal institutions. Their persistence depends not only on what networks do, but also on how they are structured and the relationships connecting their members.

Core concepts

INSTITUTIONS

Formal institutions

Official rules and structures, including laws, regulations and established organisational procedures.

INSTITUTIONS

Informal institutions

Unwritten rules, norms and practices guiding behaviour outside or alongside formal regulations.

MECHANISM

Informal networks

Personal relationships through which individuals exchange information, resources, favours, support and opportunities.

RELATIONSHIP

Mutualistic symbiosis

Formal and informal institutions coexist and influence one another, rather than simply competing.

FUNCTION

Dual functionality

Networks transmit existing practices while transforming them to accommodate new circumstances.

PROCESS

Autocatalysis

A self-reinforcing process through which interactions within a network reproduce and sustain the network itself.

1. Beyond the formal versus informal distinction

The authors challenge the assumption that informal institutions are simply substitutes for weak formal ones. They identify three ways of understanding the relationship between formal and informal institutions.

PERSPECTIVE 1

Parasitic symbiosis

Informal practices benefit from weaknesses in formal institutions. As formal institutions improve, informal practices should theoretically decline.

PERSPECTIVE 2

Commensalistic symbiosis

Informal practices emerge before formal rules and can become formalised. Formal institutions develop without necessarily eliminating existing informal practices.

THE AUTHORS' PROPOSAL

Mutualistic symbiosis

Formal and informal institutions coexist and influence one another. Institutional change can reshape informality without replacing it.

Formal institutions

Official rules, regulations, organisational procedures and institutional reforms.

⇄

Informal institutions

Personal relationships, unwritten norms, conventions and established practices.

Mutualistic symbiosis

Both institutional forms coexist, interact and adapt to one another.

2. Networks as both pipes and prisms

Informal networks are the mechanism through which informal institutions survive. Their two interconnected functions explain how practices can remain recognisable while adapting to new conditions.

↗

Pipes · Transmission

Networks channel resources, favours, information and opportunities between individuals. They transmit norms, conventions and behaviours, reproducing established informal institutions.

◇

Prisms · Transformation

Networks shape how individuals interpret and respond to institutional changes. They allow existing practices to adjust to new formal and social conditions.

Autocatalysis: why the process reinforces itself

Repeated interactions and feedback loops allow networks to reproduce themselves. These interactions sustain relationships and practices while also facilitating adaptation. Transmission and transformation therefore operate together.

3. What makes networks persistent?

The authors distinguish networks along two dimensions: how open or closed their membership is, and whether their relationships are primarily affective or instrumental.

AFFECTIVE
Trust, identity, attachment
INSTRUMENTAL
Practical interests, objectives
CLOSED
Restricted membership

Solidarity

Kinship, belonging and strong mutual obligations. Particularly resistant to change.

Domination

Restricted access, elite connections and the pursuit of influence or advantage.

OPEN
Wider membership

Redistribution

Wider relationships through which individuals exchange support and assistance.

Market exchange

Practical connections and the exchange of opportunities or resources.

The key theoretical distinction

Closed networks restrict access and reinforce existing obligations. Affective relationships create trust, identity and reciprocal responsibilities. Together, these characteristics make informal practices particularly durable. Open, instrumental networks are generally more adaptable and less resistant to change.

4. Illustrative cases

The authors use examples from different contexts to demonstrate how informal practices can survive institutional transformation.

South Korea

Yongo networks
REPRODUCTION

Networks based on kinship, seniority and Confucian values reproduce established relationships. Even with formal gender-equality legislation, male-dominated networks can preserve traditional expectations in recruitment and promotion.

Russia

Blat → Svyazi
ADAPTATION

Following the collapse of the Soviet Union, networks associated with blat adapted to the new institutional environment through newer forms of useful personal connections, commonly described as svyazi.

Kazakhstan

Rushyldyq
CONTINUITY

Clan-based loyalty survived Soviet attempts to suppress it by becoming less visible, while continuing to influence access to economic, social and political opportunities.

5. The two propositions

PROPOSITION 01

Persistence through dual functionality

Informal institutions persist because networks simultaneously adapt to changes introduced by formal institutions and preserve existing informal practices.

PROPOSITION 02

Persistence through network structure

Persistence is stronger when informal institutions are enacted through relatively closed and affective networks rather than relatively open and instrumental ones.

Methodology

The paper is theoretical, combining an interdisciplinary literature review, a conceptual framework and qualitative illustrations. Its examples draw heavily on the Global Encyclopedia of Informality, a collaborative project involving approximately 250 researchers, covering 66 countries and documenting almost 200 examples of informal practices.

Overall takeaway

Informal institutions do not necessarily disappear when formal institutions improve. Personal networks allow informal practices to survive by transmitting established norms while adapting them to changing circumstances. Their persistence is strongest when relationships are closed, affective and embedded in reciprocal obligations.
