Legacies and Leverage

EU Political Conditionality and Democracy Promotion in Historical Perspective

Frank Schimmelfennig & Hanno Scholtz · 2010 · Europe-Asia Studies, 62(3), pp. 443–460
Central argument

Historical legacies influence democratic development, but they do not determine its outcome. Credible EU incentives, particularly the prospect of membership, have an independent positive relationship with democratisation and can compensate for unfavourable historical conditions.

Core concepts

Political conditionalityExternal rewards, such as financial assistance, association or EU membership, are made dependent on compliance with democratic reforms.
External incentives modelExternal actors influence domestic political behaviour by offering rewards in exchange for compliance with specified conditions.
Credibility of conditionalityThe extent to which external actors demonstrate a genuine willingness to deliver promised rewards or withhold them when conditions are not met.
Historical legaciesInherited cultural and institutional characteristics that influence contemporary political development.
Democratic consolidationThe establishment and sustained reproduction of democratic institutions and practices.
Transnational exchangeCross-border interactions, including trade, investment, tourism and communication, that facilitate democratic diffusion and development.

Three mechanisms of democratisation

The authors distinguish three mechanisms, differing in whether their influence is political or societal, and whether it originates domestically or internationally.

01 · INTERNATIONAL / POLITICAL

EU political conditionality

The EU offers assistance, association or membership in exchange for democratic reforms. Effectiveness depends on the size and credibility of the reward. Membership is particularly powerful because its benefits can compensate governments for the political costs of reform.

TOP-DOWN INFLUENCE
02 · DOMESTIC / SOCIETAL

Economic development

Following modernisation theory, prosperity, education, urbanisation and middle-class expansion create favourable conditions for democracy by reducing poverty and encouraging political tolerance.

BOTTOM-UP INFLUENCE
03 · INTERNATIONAL / SOCIETAL

Transnational exchange

Cross-border trade, investment, communication and cultural exchanges facilitate democratic diffusion, strengthen domestic opposition and increase exposure to democratic ideas.

BOTTOM-UP INFLUENCE

Historical legacies: four explanatory variables

Historical legacies may function as independent determinants of democratisation or moderate the effectiveness of contemporary external incentives.

Religious civilisation

Western Christian, Orthodox and Muslim backgrounds are expected to produce different democratic predispositions, with Western countries considered most favourable.

Imperial tradition

Western and Central European imperial legacies are expected to facilitate democracy, whereas Russian and Ottoman legacies are expected to inhibit it.

Independent statehood

Longer experience of independent statehood is expected to facilitate democratic development.

Leninist legacies

Longer and more direct exposure to Soviet communist rule is expected to obstruct democratisation.

The relationship: legacies and leverage

The explanatory approaches are not mutually exclusive.

Historical conditions

Inherited religious, imperial, statehood and Leninist legacies shape the starting conditions for democratic development.

Contemporary incentives

Credible external rewards, especially EU membership prospects, provide governments with incentives to undertake democratic reforms.

Independent influence

Historical legacies retain explanatory importance even when contemporary EU incentives are taken into account.

Moderating influence

Historical legacies can reduce the strength of external incentives, but they do not necessarily eliminate their effect.

Combined explanation: historical legacies influence the difficulty of democratisation, while credible EU conditionality can still make an independent contribution.

Research design

The study covers the Eastern and Mediterranean neighbourhoods of the EU. The authors employ panel regression and use Freedom House Political Rights ratings, reversed to a 0–6 scale, to measure democracy.

Empirical findings

EU conditionality: Higher levels of conditional incentives are generally associated with higher subsequent democracy levels. Credible membership incentives demonstrate a robust positive relationship.

Religious civilisation: The strongest and most consistent historical legacy. Western Christian countries demonstrate higher democracy levels than Orthodox countries, which outperform Muslim countries.

Imperial legacies: Western and Central European imperial traditions positively correlate with democracy, but have relatively little impact on the significance of EU conditionality.

Independent statehood: Earlier statehood generally correlates positively with democracy, although the relationship is less consistent.

Leninist legacies: Later incorporation into the Soviet Union or indirect Soviet rule is associated with higher democracy levels than early Soviet incorporation.

The combined models show that historical legacies reduce the strength of EU conditionality's effects, but do not eliminate their statistical significance.

Conclusions and implications

Historical legacies and external incentives are complementary explanations. Religious civilisation represents the strongest historical influence, but EU membership conditionality retains an independent contribution to democratisation.

The EU's transformative potential therefore depends partly on its willingness to extend credible membership prospects beyond countries with favourable Western European historical backgrounds.

Core takeaway

Historical legacies shape the starting conditions and difficulty of democratisation, but they do not determine its outcome. Credible EU membership incentives can overcome historical disadvantages and independently facilitate democratic development.
