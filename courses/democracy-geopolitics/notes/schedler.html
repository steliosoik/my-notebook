Shaping the Authoritarian Arena

The Politics of Uncertainty: Sustaining and Subverting Electoral Authoritarianism · Chapter 2

Andreas Schedler · 2013 · pp. 54–76
Central argument

Electoral authoritarian regimes do not survive by simply eliminating democracy's institutional architecture. They manipulate representative institutions to preserve authoritarian control. Yet these institutions cannot be completely controlled without losing their governing utility. They therefore remain spaces in which opposition and resistance can emerge.

Core concepts

Electoral authoritarianismA regime that formally adopts democratic institutions while systematically manipulating their operation to preserve authoritarian rule.
Institutions of dominationOrganisational structures through which authoritarian rulers establish control using violence, money and ideology.
Institutions of representationFormal institutions intended to constrain state power and facilitate political pluralism, including legislatures, courts, elections, parties and independent media.
Institutional manipulationPurposeful interference by authoritarian rulers with formally representative institutions to prevent them from developing meaningful autonomy.
Institutional landscapingMacro-level decisions concerning the overall institutional architecture of an authoritarian regime.
Institutional gardeningContinuous, micro-level adjustments and manipulation of existing institutions to maintain authoritarian control.
Institutional ambivalenceThe inherent duality of representative institutions, which serve authoritarian interests while simultaneously creating opportunities for opposition and resistance.

The institutional foundations of authoritarianism

Schedler distinguishes between two institutional logics. Electoral authoritarian regimes combine structures that sustain authoritarian domination with institutions that formally constrain it.

Institutions of domination

Preserving authoritarian control

Structures through which rulers exercise power, restrict challenges and sustain regime authority.

Institutions of representation

Constraining state power

Formal democratic institutions intended to divide power and introduce societal pluralism.

Three resources sustain authoritarian domination:

ViolenceGenerates fear and is institutionalised through repression and repressive bureaucracies.
MoneySatisfies material interests and facilitates co-optation through economic institutions.
IdeologyEstablishes regime-supportive beliefs and legitimates authoritarian rule through ideological apparatuses.
Violence → Repression
Money → Co-optation
Ideology → Legitimation

The two stages of democratisation

Schedler distinguishes between establishing representative institutions formally and making them effective in practice. Contemporary electoral autocracies have largely conceded the first battle while attempting to win the second through manipulation.

STAGE 01

De jure representation

Establishing representative institutions formally, through constitutional and legal arrangements.

STAGE 02

De facto representation

Making those institutions effective in practice, with meaningful autonomy and the capacity to constrain power.

Dividing power within the state

Legislatures, courts and decentralised authorities distribute authority and formally constrain executive power.

Introducing societal pluralism

Elections, parties, media and civil society create channels for political competition and social representation.

Institutional manipulation: containing agents and adversaries

Authoritarian rulers manipulate two categories of actors. They seek to control both those operating within state institutions and those capable of organising opposition outside them.

Containing agents

Controlling institutions that exercise delegated state authority.

  • Legislative disempowerment
  • Controlling the selection of representatives
  • Institutional fragmentation
  • Judicial insulation
  • Bureaucratic oversight of local authorities

Containing adversaries

Restricting societal pluralism and the capacity for independent organisation.

  • Media ownership control, censorship and intimidation
  • Hierarchical state-controlled organisations
  • Societal disorganisation
  • Divide et impera: exploiting divisions between independent organisations through selective rewards and punishments
Institutions are difficult to abolish once established. Rulers therefore continually adapt their manipulation strategies rather than dismantling the institutional architecture.

New institutionalism and institutional ambivalence

Schedler challenges the assumption that representative institutions are merely decorative under authoritarianism. New institutionalist scholarship demonstrates their instrumental value: legislatures, parties and courts facilitate co-optation, power-sharing, conflict management and regime survival.

The same institutions produce two opposing effects

Instruments of authoritarian rule

Representative institutions help rulers manage conflict, co-opt political actors, distribute power and maintain regime survival.

Arenas of resistance

Institutions empower regime agents and potential adversaries, who can develop interests and sources of autonomy independent of the ruler.

The institutional dilemma: Institutions require some autonomy to perform their governing functions. That same autonomy creates opportunities for subversion.
Core takeaway

Electoral authoritarianism survives not by eliminating democracy's institutional architecture but by manipulating it. Nevertheless, institutional manipulation is never absolute. Representative institutions remain arenas of struggle, simultaneously facilitating authoritarian domination and creating opportunities for democratic resistance.
