DEMOCRACY & GEOPOLITICS / COURSE MATERIALS

Course syllabus

Course structure, learning objectives, assessment and weekly reading programme for Block 2, 2026–2027.

UNIVERSITYLeiden University
INSTITUTEInstitute of Political Science
INSTRUCTORAmy Eaglestone

01 / COURSE DESCRIPTION

Democracy in a geopolitical space

Since the end of the Cold War, former Soviet states with democratic aspirations, including Moldova, Georgia and Ukraine, have navigated a geopolitical space between the West and Russia. Both Europe and Russia seek to protect their security and economic interests by influencing domestic actors and decision-making in Eastern Europe.

The course explores how democracy fares under geopolitical tensions in Europe's Eastern Neighbourhood, considering historical legacies, informal political cultures, institutional designs and the contemporary international environment.

02 / LEARNING OUTCOMES

What you should be able to do

  • Understand current academic debates and research on the challenges faced by democracy in the Eastern European Neighbourhood.
  • Apply concepts and theories about democracy and geopolitics to different country cases.
  • Evaluate the impact of core mechanisms on democratic trajectories and discuss potential strategies for governments navigating geopolitical space.

03 / ASSESSMENT

How the course is assessed

40%Final exam

Historical legacies

Domestic factors, historical legacies in former Soviet states and the effects of informality on democracy.

  • Hale (2015), Chapter 2: “Patronal Politics and the Great Power of Expectations.”

5 NOVEMBER 2026 · SESSION 4Theory

Geopolitical tensions, societal and political divides, ideology and ethnicity in the former Soviet space.

  • Enyedi (2005), “The Role of Agency in Cleavage Formation.”

05 / COURSE REQUIREMENTS

Important assessment rules

  • Attendance is strongly advised because of the interactive nature of the course and in-class assessments.
  • Group presentations should last 25–30 minutes and include theory, country-case analysis, potential strategies and key takeaways.
  • Presentation slides must be submitted to Brightspace 24 hours before the presentation.
  • Student-led sessions last 45 minutes. A draft programme must be shared with the instructor 48 hours beforehand.
  • The exam covers all literature discussed in class and may include open, multiple-choice and short-answer questions.
  • Late submissions are not accepted unless prior permission is obtained. Exam resits are available.
